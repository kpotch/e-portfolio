Reflections

This is where I connect the dots between what's on the Projects page and where I'm headed — why I chose what I included, what it taught me, and how it points toward SOC analyst work.

Why these artifacts

Updated November 3, 2026

I chose artifacts that show two sides of the same skill set. The Global Career Accelerator projects, including the Excel, SQL, and Tableau analyses for organizations like Intel, the Grammys, and Transport for London, show that I can take a messy, open-ended dataset, ask useful questions of it, and turn the results into a recommendation someone could act on.

The security labs and coursework show the foundation underneath. The Nmap, reconnaissance, threat modeling, and password cracking labs show how I think about systems from an attacker's point of view and then from a defender's. The policy paper, event report, and program review show that I also care about the legal, ethical, and human side of protecting information. What ties everything together is working with evidence: whether I'm reading query results or scan output, I try to tie every conclusion back to what the data actually shows.

What I'm still building toward

Updated November 3, 2026

My goal is to work as a SOC analyst, and I'm honest about the gap. My hands-on security work so far has happened in controlled environments: my own virtual lab, course labs, and the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate. I have not yet triaged alerts from a live production environment or worked a real incident, and that is the experience I'm looking for in an entry-level role.

What I can bring right now is a solid grounding in networking, operating systems, and security fundamentals, plus data skills that many entry-level candidates don't have. Querying large datasets, spotting patterns, and explaining findings clearly are exactly the habits that log analysis and alert triage depend on. Between now and graduation in December 2026, I'm continuing to add labs and projects here and sharpening my résumé so the gap keeps getting smaller.

How this portfolio will grow

Updated November 3, 2026

I built this portfolio in stages over the semester rather than all at once. It started with a handful of internship dashboards and Python programs. Since then I've added the rest of my Global Career Accelerator analyses, my security lab reports, my Information Science papers, and my certifications, each with a short description of what I did and what I learned.

As I finish more coursework and the semester wraps up, I plan to add my remaining assignments, finish the final presentation video, and update my résumé. I decide what belongs here by asking whether an artifact shows a real skill, something I could explain in an interview, and whether it moves me closer to a security role. This page will keep changing as the portfolio does.
